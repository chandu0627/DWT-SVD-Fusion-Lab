## Slide 2 - Introduction

- Image fusion combines useful information from two or more images.
- Different inputs may contain complementary structures or details.
- DWT separates an image into approximation and directional detail coefficients.
- SVD provides a low-rank representation and an energy measure for coefficient matrices.
- This project combines both methods using deterministic signal-processing rules.

**Place on slide:** Two example grayscale inputs next to a fused output, captured from the application.

## Slide 3 - Problem Statement

- Two images may have different dimensions or color formats.
- Direct pixelwise averaging can soften strong details and treats every region equally.
- A fusion method should combine coarse structure and local details using suitable rules.
- The output must be reconstructed as a valid grayscale image with consistent dimensions.
- Fusion quality should be summarized with measurable image metrics.

## Slide 4 - Objectives

- Accept two uploaded grayscale or RGB images.
- Convert inputs to grayscale, normalize them, and resize them to a common size.
- Decompose both images using a selectable 2D wavelet and level.
- Fuse approximation and detail coefficients using the selected method.

## Slide 5 - Existing System

- Basic fusion methods often use direct averaging or fixed pixelwise combinations.
- Direct averaging does not distinguish coarse image structure from fine detail.
- Strong edges may be weakened when dissimilar pixels are averaged.
- Hand-designed rules can be simple and interpretable but depend on their chosen coefficients.
- This project uses DWT and SVD rules rather than learned models.

**Place on slide:** A visual comparison of two inputs and a simple average generated for the presentation, clearly marked as an illustrative baseline.

## Slide 6 - Proposed System

- Convert both images to normalized grayscale and align their dimensions.
- Apply multilevel DWT to obtain the approximation and three detail bands.
- Use truncated SVD and retained energy to blend the LL approximation bands.
- By default, choose each detail coefficient with the larger absolute value.
- Reconstruct with inverse DWT and report metrics against each aligned input.

## Slide 8 - DWT

- DWT represents an image at multiple spatial scales.
- LL is the approximation band and contains coarse image structure.
- LH, HL, and HH are directional detail bands containing edges and texture information.
- The application uses PyWavelets `wavedec2` and supports selectable wavelet families.
- The requested decomposition level is limited to the level supported by the image and wavelet.

**Place on slide:** The app's four-band visualization for one input, with LL, LH, HL, and HH labeled.

## Slide 9 - SVD

- SVD factors a matrix as $A = U\Sigma V^T$.
- The diagonal entries of $\Sigma$ are singular values ordered by magnitude.
- Larger singular values contribute more to the matrix's squared Frobenius energy.
- The application retains the leading 10% of singular values, with at least one retained value.
- The retained values form a low-rank approximation and provide the fusion weights.

**Place on slide:** The application's LL singular-value spectrum for both inputs; label rank and retained-energy ratios from that run.

## Slide 10 - Proposed Fusion Method

- Decompose both aligned grayscale inputs with the same wavelet and level.
- Apply truncated SVD to each LL approximation matrix.
- Weight the two low-rank LL reconstructions by their retained singular-value energies.
- In the default mode, select the stronger absolute coefficient at each LH, HL, and HH location.
- An optional mode applies the energy-weighted SVD method to all coefficient bands.

**Place on slide:** Equations for the two SVDs and weighted LL fusion, alongside a small coefficient-band fusion sketch.

## Slide 11 - System Architecture

- The user supplies Image 1 and Image 2 through the Streamlit interface.
- Preprocessing produces grayscale normalized arrays with a common shape.
- DWT generates a coefficient pyramid for each input.
- The fusion stage creates a fused coefficient pyramid using the selected method.
- Inverse DWT produces the output, which is evaluated and made available for download.

## Slide 12 - Implementation

- `preprocessing.py` decodes uploads, converts color images to grayscale, normalizes, and resizes.
- `dwt_fusion.py` performs decomposition, coefficient fusion, and coefficient summaries.
- `svd_fusion.py` calculates truncated SVD reconstructions and retained energy.
- `reconstruction.py` applies inverse DWT and returns an 8-bit image of the requested dimensions.
- `metrics.py` calculates MSE, PSNR, SSIM, and fused-image entropy.

## Slide 14 - Performance Metrics

- MSE measures mean squared intensity difference; lower means closer pixel values.
- PSNR expresses reconstruction error in decibels; higher means lower error.
- SSIM compares local luminance, contrast, and structure; values closer to 1 indicate greater similarity.
- Entropy measures the intensity distribution of the fused output; it is not by itself a quality score.
- Record metrics against Image 1 and Image 2 separately; SSIM is unavailable for images smaller than 3 × 3.

- Medical and scientific imaging can use fusion to compare complementary modalities.

## Slide 16 - Advantages and Limitations

- Advantage: the processing stages and coefficient rules are interpretable.
- Advantage: the method requires no training data, model training, or GPU.
- Limitation: RGB inputs are converted to grayscale, so color is discarded.
- Limitation: resizing can distort aspect ratio and may affect metric values.
- Limitation: maximum-absolute detail selection can also preserve noise; all-band SVD costs more computation.
